# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two overlapping audiences with different contexts:

- **AI researchers and security professionals** — methodical, technically fluent, filing structured vulnerability reports. They arrive knowing what they found and want an efficient, credible channel to route it correctly.
- **General public** — non-technical people who encountered an AI harm or failure. They may not know the right terminology, may feel uncertain whether their experience "counts," and need reassurance that reporting is worth their time.

Both audiences share one condition: they are acting on something that matters to them. The urgency level varies by report type, not by user type.

## Product Purpose

FLARE-AI is an open-source tool for reporting AI flaws and incidents, built by researchers to connect issues with the people who can fix them. It integrates with CERT/VINCE to route reports to appropriate parties. The measure of success is that a report filed today reaches the right hands and contributes to safer AI systems.

## Brand Personality

Open-source, trustworthy, credible.

The interface should feel like it was built by domain experts who also care about usability — not assembled from a template, not enterprise-polished into blandness. The tone is calm and direct: serious about the subject, easy to navigate.

## Anti-references

Not a corporate enterprise security tool that feels vibecoded. Avoid the aesthetic of: generic SaaS security dashboards with dark gradients and glowing accents, government portals that signal bureaucratic friction, startup tools with bubbly illustrations and "gamified" copy. The tool is built by researchers, for researchers and the public — it should feel like that provenance shows.

## Design Principles

1. **Serve both audiences without condescension.** The form must be comprehensible to a first-time reporter and efficient for a security professional. Never dumb things down with excessive hand-holding, but never assume jargon fluency either.
2. **Credibility through structure, not marketing.** Trust is built by the quality of the interface — clear routing, logical field order, honest labels — not by copy that promises your report matters. Show it, don't say it.
3. **Friction is earned.** Every field, step, and interaction must justify its presence. The reporter is doing the user a favor by submitting; the tool owes them the shortest path to done.
4. **Open-source legibility.** The design should feel intentional and domain-specific — like it came from people who understand AI safety, not a design agency handed a brief.
5. **Calm urgency.** The tone holds the weight of what's being reported without becoming alarming. Neither dismissive of the reporter's concern nor overwrought about it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Design for the full user range: non-technical reporters may have lower digital literacy; researchers may be under time pressure. Ensure reduced motion support and sufficient color contrast across both light and dark themes.
